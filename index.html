The Husbandry Problem

Optimizing Beef Production with Derivative Dynamics

In intensive Malaysian feedlots, managing feed formulation (such as oil palm frond silage and palm kernel cake) requires knowing the exact transition point from bone/muscle development to fat accumulation.

By calculating the **first derivative $f'(x)$** (Average Daily Gain), we can identify peak metabolism efficiency. The **second derivative $f''(x)$** tells us when acceleration slows down, signaling when keeping the cattle on feed results in diminishing economic returns.

Bio-Calculus Model Deck

Cattle Growth Logistic Function:
$$f(x) = \frac{A}{1 + B e^{-kx}}$$

Yields cumulative weight (kg) at age x (months). A is mature weight, B is the birth-scale constant, and k is the maturing rate.

First Derivative (Instantaneous Growth Velocity):
$$f'(x) = \frac{A \cdot B \cdot k \cdot e^{-kx}}{(1 + B e^{-kx})^2}$$

Yields weight change rate per month ($\text{kg/month}$). To convert to standard husbandry metric **Average Daily Gain (ADG)**, we compute: $$\text{ADG (g/day)} = \frac{f'(x) \times 1000}{30.4}$$
